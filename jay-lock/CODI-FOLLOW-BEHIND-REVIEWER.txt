PROJECT MISSION
---------------
The game is a stylized 2D side-view cutaway oil-rig survival/management game.
The station is autonomous and machine-driven; it does not require a human crew.
The player helps the station AI operate, maintain, repair, and expand the rig.
The experience centers on industrial machinery, energy and resources,
underwater exploration, an autonomous deep-sea submarine, and consequences
for failures and resource shortages.

This is a fictional game system, not a realistic oil-rig simulator.

THE OWNER'S CORE DESIGN VALUES
------------------------------
1. Preserve the established game vision and existing project architecture.
2. Keep the station readable, industrial, alive, and visually coherent.
3. Add depth through meaningful machinery, pipes, gauges, energy readouts,
   layered geometry, lighting, and simple animations.
4. Keep mechanics consequential: damage, repairs, power shortages, fuel use,
   resource scarcity, and upgrades should matter.
5. Favor modular, reusable, low-cost 2D art and simple animations that work
   well in Godot.
6. Do not invent unapproved mechanics, economic formulas, or structural changes.
7. The owner's decisions are the final authority. If a design decision is unclear,
   ask or present a clearly labeled proposal instead of silently choosing.

VISUAL STYLE LOCK
-----------------
The established art is a crisp, flat-shaded 2D side-view cutaway:
- Two visible interior floors with the front wall removed.
- Industrial steel framing, ribs, floor plates, machinery, pipes, tanks,
  cabinets, pumps, generators, workstations, and storage.
- Dark steel/grey structure, yellow safety markings, orange flotation modules,
  blue/cyan utility lines and AI technology, small warning lamps, and warm lights.
- A central raised AI/control module with a bright blue/cyan core.
- Strong silhouettes and readable details: vents, fasteners, service panels,
  gauges, pipe clamps, couplings, valves, brackets, handles, and displays.
- Add a little visual polish at a time; do not replace the established style.

Avoid sparse rooms made from generic rectangles, shapes that look pasted over
a background, photorealistic over-rendering, random clutter, and a continuous
orange slab across the entire underside.

LOCKED UNDERDECK / SUBMARINE HATCH DESIGN
-----------------------------------------
The blue central underside section is the submarine launch/recovery hatch.
It is NOT a ballast tank, flotation compartment, or generic blue panel.

Required visual treatment:
- A framed, sealed opening in the keel/underside.
- Visible locking blocks, pressure-seal edges, actuators or sliding mechanisms,
  hazard markings, guide rails, hydraulic tubes, service pipes, and status lamps.
- The hatch must look integrated into the station structure.
- Keep the submarine's vertical path from the hatch into the water unobstructed.
- Do not place pontoons, braces, pipes, or decorative details across the path.

PONTOONS:
- The current design direction uses two separate orange outer pontoons, one on
  the left and one on the right.
- Keep visible open space between them.
- Do not join them into a continuous orange bottom.
- Keep the hatch centered between the outer pontoons.
- Do not add central pontoons or alter their layout without owner approval.

HATCH AND SUBMARINE GAMEPLAY EXPECTATIONS
-----------------------------------------
The hatch should have coherent states such as closed, unlocking, opening, open,
closing, and jammed. The submarine should distinguish docked, preparing,
deploying, away, returning, recovering, and disabled where appropriate.

Expected rules:
- The submarine cannot launch through a closed hatch.
- The hatch cannot close while the submarine is outside the bay.
- Recovery requires an appropriate open hatch and sufficient power.
- Opening/closing and launch/recovery take time and may consume resources.
- Damage can jam systems; repairs restore function.
- Visible animation and status feedback should match the actual simulation state.

Reuse the existing project's authoritative submarine, power, fuel, damage, and
resource systems. Do not create a second state machine or duplicate resource
ledger if one already exists. Any sample integration code must be adapted to
the real project's node paths, types, signals, and coordinate system.

INDUSTRIAL SYSTEM DETAIL
-----------------------
Improve readability and function through:
- Color-coded utility pipes with a consistent meaning.
- Connected pipe routes, elbows, clamps, junctions, valves, and couplings.
- Pumps, power conduits, switchgear, batteries, generators, cooling systems,
  tanks, pressure gauges, energy displays, and service panels.
- Clear powered, offline, warning, and damaged visual states.
- Simple fan/pump motion, blinking lights, flowing highlights, and hatch motion
  only where they communicate useful state.

Energy readouts must display the real simulation values. Never add decorative
numbers that disagree with gameplay state. Do not create a parallel energy or
resource simulation just to power a visual effect.
